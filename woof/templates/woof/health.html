{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Dog health & care{% endblocktranslate %}

{% include 'woof/includes/guide_disclaimer.html' %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}What this guide covers{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Dog health is a combination of prevention, observation, and consistent care. It is not only about treating illness, but about maintaining a stable physical condition throughout the dog’s life.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}This guide focuses on general principles of health management, not veterinary diagnosis or treatment. Any medical concerns should always be handled by a qualified veterinarian.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Preventive care and routine checks{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Preventive care is the most important aspect of maintaining long-term health. Regular veterinary check-ups help detect issues early, often before symptoms become visible.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Vaccinations, parasite prevention, and routine examinations form the foundation of responsible ownership. These measures reduce the risk of serious illness and improve overall lifespan quality.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Many health problems develop gradually, which is why consistent monitoring is more effective than reactive treatment.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Nutrition and physical condition{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Nutrition directly influences energy levels, coat quality, weight, and immune system stability. A balanced diet should be appropriate for the dog’s age, size, and activity level.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Overfeeding is one of the most common health issues in domestic dogs and can lead to obesity-related complications. Underfeeding or low-quality diets can also cause deficiencies and long-term problems.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Consistency in feeding habits helps maintain stable digestion and prevents sudden metabolic stress.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}For practical details — food formats, feeding by age, portions and the drinking regime — see the separate guide on feeding your dog.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Hygiene and grooming{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Grooming is not only cosmetic but also a health-related necessity. Regular brushing helps prevent skin irritation, removes dead hair, and improves blood circulation.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Nail trimming, ear cleaning, and dental care are often overlooked but essential for preventing infections and discomfort.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}The frequency of grooming depends on breed type, coat length, and lifestyle.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Common health issues{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Some health problems are more common across domestic dogs, including skin allergies, digestive issues, and joint stress in larger breeds.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Early signs often include changes in appetite, energy levels, coat condition, or behavior patterns. These indicators should not be ignored, even if they appear minor.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Timely response to early symptoms significantly improves outcomes and reduces long-term complications.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Physical activity and body maintenance{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Regular physical activity supports cardiovascular health, muscle tone, and joint mobility. The required level of activity varies significantly depending on breed and age.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Insufficient movement can lead to weight gain and reduced overall vitality, while excessive strain without proper rest can cause injuries.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}A balanced approach to exercise is essential for long-term physical stability.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Mental health and well-being{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Mental health is closely linked to physical health in dogs. Stress, boredom, and lack of stimulation can negatively affect the immune system and behavior.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Environmental stability, interaction, and predictable routines help maintain emotional balance.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}A mentally healthy dog is generally more stable, responsive, and physically resilient.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Your dog's health record{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Keep one folder — paper or digital — with everything in it: vaccination dates, deworming and parasite treatment history, microchip number, insurance policy, past test results, and a list of medications with dates. Add your dog's weight at each check-up.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}This record saves time in emergencies, makes changing clinics painless, and helps you notice slow trends — for example, gradual weight gain or increasing thirst — long before they become problems.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Dental care in practice{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Brushing two to three times a week with a dog-specific toothpaste is the gold standard. Introduce it gradually: let the dog taste the paste from your finger first, then touch the teeth and gums for a second, and build up to a full session over two to three weeks.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Never use human toothpaste — fluoride and foaming agents are not meant to be swallowed. Dental chews and additives can support the routine but do not replace brushing. Bad breath, yellow-brown tartar, or bleeding gums are reasons for a veterinary dental check.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Ears, eyes, paws and nails{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Check ears weekly: they should be pale pink inside, without dark buildup or a sour smell. Floppy-eared breeds need this check especially often. Clean with a vet-approved ear solution on a cotton pad — never cotton sticks inside the canal.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Eyes should be clear and free of crusts; wipe the corners daily with a damp cloth if discharge appears. Nails are due when they touch the floor standing on a hard surface — long nails change the dog's gait and strain the joints over time.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Between the toes, check after every walk for burrs, ice clumps in winter, or salt residue. Trim the hair between paw pads in long-haired breeds to prevent slipping on smooth floors.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Parasite prevention{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Fleas, ticks, and intestinal worms are prevented with regular products — spot-ons, tablets, or collars — prescribed by a veterinarian based on the dog's weight and lifestyle. Missed doses are the most common reason for breakouts.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}After walks in tall grass or woodland, run your hands over the dog's body: armpits, neck, groin, and ears are the favourite attachment sites. Remove a tick with a special tick tool or fine tweezers by twisting gently, then disinfect the spot and monitor the skin for a few days.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Monitoring weight and body condition{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}The simplest method is a monthly hands-on check. You should feel the ribs easily with light pressure but not see them. Viewed from above, the dog should have a visible waist tuck behind the ribs; from the side, the belly should slope upward.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Weigh large and giant breeds monthly and small breeds at every vet visit. If the ribs become hard to feel, reduce the daily ration slightly and add more sniffing-based activity; if the ribs show clearly, increase food and check for parasites or other causes.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}When to contact a vet urgently{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Do not wait for a morning appointment if you notice: repeated vomiting or diarrhoea within hours, a visibly bloated and tense abdomen especially in large deep-chested breeds, collapse or extreme weakness, difficulty breathing, sudden severe pain or screaming when touched, bloating of the abdomen with unproductive retching, a seizure lasting more than a minute, or inability to urinate in a male dog.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Also urgent: ingestion of a known poison (chocolate, grapes, xylitol, medications), eye injuries, limping that lasts more than a day, giving birth that stalls for more than an hour between puppies, or any bleeding that does not stop with light pressure.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}First-aid basics at home{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}A simple home kit saves valuable minutes: gauze pads and bandage, veterinary disinfectant, tweezers and a tick tool, a digital thermometer, saline solution, and your clinic's number saved in your phone. Learn how to measure temperature and how to bandage a paw before you need it.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}In case of poisoning, do not induce vomiting on your own — bring the packaging or a sample of the substance to the vet. In case of burns, cool the area with running water and cover it loosely. For heavy bleeding, press a clean pad firmly for several minutes without checking constantly.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Heat and cold safety{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}In summer, walk in the early morning and evening; test the pavement with the back of your hand for seven seconds — if it is too hot for your hand, it is too hot for the paws. Never leave a dog in a parked car: even a mild outside temperature quickly becomes fatal inside.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Signs of overheating are heavy panting, salivation, weakness, and vomiting — move the dog into shade or air conditioning, cool with cool (not ice-cold) water, and contact a vet. In cold weather, short-haired and small dogs benefit from a well-fitting coat; wipe paws after walks to remove road salt.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Senior dog care{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Senior dogs benefit from check-ups every six months rather than annually: blood and urine tests catch kidney, liver, and hormonal changes early, while they are still manageable with diet and lifestyle.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}At home, add orthopaedic bedding, a non-slip runner on smooth floors, and a ramp for the car or sofa if climbing becomes difficult. Slow walks with plenty of sniffing keep joints and mind in better shape than long demanding marches.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Conclusion{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Dog health is a continuous process rather than a fixed state. It requires consistent attention to nutrition, hygiene, prevention, and lifestyle balance.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}When properly managed, these factors significantly increase both the quality and length of a dog’s life.{% endblocktranslate %}
