{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Feeding your dog{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}What this guide covers{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Feeding is one of the most important parts of everyday dog care. This guide explains general feeding principles that apply to any healthy dog: food formats, portions, feeding by age and a proper drinking regime.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}This guide deliberately avoids brand names. Every dog is different: the final ration always depends on age, size, activity level and health condition.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Dry and wet food{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Dry food is convenient: it is easy to store, measure and dose. It stays fresh longer, and its crunchy texture helps clean teeth mechanically.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Wet food contains much more moisture, which helps maintain water balance. It is often more palatable, which suits picky eaters, older dogs and dogs with dental problems.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Both formats can be combined. What matters most is that the ration is complete and balanced for the dog's age and size. An opened tin of wet food must be stored in the refrigerator and used within a short time.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}How to choose food{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Read the composition: good food lists a specific source of protein first — chicken, beef or fish — instead of vague mixtures.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Consider the age category, the size of the kibble for small breeds, and special needs such as allergies or sensitive digestion.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Avoid products with unclear composition, excess salt, sugar or artificial colours. If in doubt, ask your veterinarian.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Feeding across ages{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Puppies need small frequent meals: roughly three to four times a day, gradually reduced to two meals by the age of six to twelve months.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Adult dogs usually feel best with one or two regular meals a day at the same time.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Senior dogs may need smaller portions, easier-to-digest food and, often, more frequent feeding. During pregnancy and lactation the dog needs more food and more frequent meals.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Portions and schedule{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}The portion depends on weight, activity and the food itself. Use the feeding table on the package as a starting point and adjust it by observing the dog's condition.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Regular meals at fixed times help digestion and prevent begging. For adult dogs avoid free access to the bowl all day — it often leads to obesity.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Treats should make up no more than ten percent of the daily calories.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Drinking regime{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Fresh water must be available at all times, not only during meals.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Change the water daily and wash the bowl regularly. In hot weather, after activity or when eating dry food, the dog drinks noticeably more.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}A sudden increase or decrease in thirst can be a sign of health problems — in that case, consult a veterinarian.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Switching food{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Change the food gradually, over seven to ten days, mixing the new food into the old one in increasing proportions.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}A sudden change often causes digestive upset: loose stool, refusal of food or bloating. Watch your dog carefully during the transition.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Foods to avoid{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Some human foods are dangerous for dogs: chocolate, grapes and raisins, onions and garlic, xylitol, alcohol and cooked bones.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Salty, spicy and fatty food from the table is also best avoided. Keep these products out of the dog's reach.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Conclusion{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}Feeding is a routine, but a conscious one: a stable schedule, a quality ration and constant access to water are the foundation of health.{% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %}If you are not sure about the right food for your dog, always consult a specialist — the right choice is easier to make together.{% endblocktranslate %}
